# Horders: world bible and voice guide

*Design, 27 Nov 2026. Checkpoint 1 of the vision v2 plan ([roadmap.md](roadmap.md), "1.0: the road to launch"; [vision/vision.md](vision/vision.md) section 3). Pillar 5: one world, one voice, honestly made.*

**Status: proposed.** Every player-facing name and line here waits for Steve's yes (roadmap, pillar 5). Until then nothing in this file changes the game. New names are in [rename-map.md](rename-map.md), with the searches behind them. Ids in code never change; only display text does.

**How to use this file.** Any PR that adds or changes a name, a line of text, a foe, a place or a sound checks it against this page first and says so in the PR body. If something here and the roadmap disagree, the roadmap wins and this page gets fixed.

## Part 1: the world bible

### The world in one paragraph

The surface has gone cold. Long ago a family of fire-kings and fire-mothers, **the Furnace Court**, pulled every flame in the land down into one mountain-sized furnace, **the Hoard**, and sealed themselves inside with it. They did not need it. They wanted it. Above, the last town, **Wick**, huddles round one guttering hearth. You are a **delver** from Wick. You go down into the Hoard, steal fire from the Court and carry it home as **embers**. Each ember you bring back keeps Wick alive one more night and lights one more of its buildings. The Court notices. The Court minds.

### Three facts every writer must know

1. **The Court are hoarders, not monsters.** They are greedy, grand and very old. They talk down to you. They are a little funny because they are so sure of themselves. They are never sad, never tragic, and never explained at length.
2. **Fire is the only thing that matters.** Embers are stolen fire. Your class's power is fire put to work. Water is the one thing the Court fears, which is why every interrupt is called **Quench**. Cold is what happens when fire is lost.
3. **Clay against iron.** *Everything the Court made is fired clay*: terracotta, glaze and stoneware, glowing through cracks, and it shatters when it breaks. *Everything Wick made is iron, cloth and brass*: dented, patched, warm. If you can't tell which side something is on by its material, it is designed wrong. (Art: [vision/proposal-art.md](vision/proposal-art.md). Sound: clay, iron, fire, water and steam, glass and bells.)

A family, not an army. The members below are the bosses. Their creatures are fired by the Kiln Mother and sent where they are needed. Names marked *new* are proposals from the rename map.

**The rule for Court names:** a family title plus one plain English word tied to what they do (Aunt Ebb, Uncle Glut, Cousin Stamp), or "The" plus one plain word plus the title (The Kiln Mother, The Glass Heir). Titles in use: Grandmother, Mother, Regent, Dowager, Heir, Queen, Aunt, Uncle, Cousin. No invented personal names.

### Wick, the last town

Wick is one fixed scene at night: a square of iron-roofed houses round **the Hearth**, with the mouth of the Hoard at the bottom of the hill. It is the hub. Buildings stay dark until their system unlocks, and each one is one system:

| Building | What it holds |
|---|---|
| **The Hearth** | The middle of town and the Play button. It burns brighter as you bring embers home. |
| **The Forge** | Gear upgrades, paid in embers (the one currency sink). |
| **The Notice Board** | Quick Delve (the daily seed) and the Weekly Cache. |
| **The Journal** (kept by the Hearthkeeper) | Where each Treasure drops, foes met, titles earned. From hour 2. |
| **The trade halls** | One per class, lit as each trade unlocks. The talent tree lives here. |

**People of Wick.** The **Hearthkeeper** is an old woman who kept the last coal alight. She is the only voice in town: dry, warm, short with words. Bots who fill your party are Wick folk with a trade and a first name ("Anvil-smith Brenna", "Coal-carrier Tam"), never "Hero 2". First names are short, old, ordinary ones: Brenna, Tam, Hal, Edda, Wyn, Pell, Ruth, Mags, Ossie, Jory, Nell, Abe. Never a famous fantasy or Warcraft name.

### The layers of the Hoard (places)

The Hoard goes down in layers. Each place is one layer, has one Court member, one landmark room, two accent colours on top of the shared palette ([vision/vision.md](vision/vision.md) section 7), and its own foes (no two places share a trash list, roadmap). The **launch places** are the five solo layers and the Hammerdeep, plus the Strongroom if Checkpoint 5 is on time.

| Place | Layer | One line | What the floor does |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Kiln Depths** | The mouth | Where the Kiln Mother fires her pots. Hot, close, orange through cracks. | Fire on the floor, gold safe spots |
| **The Drowned Foundry** | Second | The river broke in and drowned the Court's bellows-works. Steam, standing water, sunk machinery. | Steam vents boil off Temper |
| **The Glarewell** | Third | The lens-tower where the Court watches the cold world above. White glare, black shadow, pillars. | Hide from the Glare behind pillars |
| **The Coldmarrow** | The bottom | Where even the Court's fire died. Frost on clay. Your coal is the only light. | Keep your coal alight |
| **The Wrackline** | Off the side | The underground quay where stolen fire was shipped in. Rafts, barges, black water. | Stay on your raft when the Ebb pulls |
| **The Hammerdeep** | Group dungeon | The Iron Uncle's armoury of stolen Wick iron, the one place below with iron in it. | Tank holds, party stays behind |
| **The Strongroom** *(new; the Hoard raid)* | The vault | Where Uncle Glut keeps (and eats) the Court's embers. The goal of the whole game. | Raid: roles, swallowed allies |
| **The Ashvents** | Held (a season) | Twin vents where Cousin Fork burns in two places at once. | Fill each gold vent |
| **The Pressworks** | Held (a season) | Cousin Stamp's presses, where the Court's mark is struck. | Take turns under the press |
| **The Crucible** | Held (raid) | Grandmother Slag's own pot, the oldest fire in the Hoard. | Molten lanes, a ladle |
| **The Smeltery** | Held (raid) | Where the Colossus was fired and still stands. | Hit its back, not its plates |
| **The Flue** | Held (raid) | The great chimney the Regent rules from. | Pass the Decree |

"The Hoard" means the whole furnace and nothing else. The raid that used to share the name is the Strongroom (rename map).

One word, one meaning (vision rule 5). Each system has a reason to exist in the world. Words marked *new* are rename-map proposals.

| Word | What it is in the world |
|---|---|

## Part 2: the voice guide

### Three voices

| Voice | Who | Sounds like | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Court** | Boss taunts (about 100, chosen by what happened in the run) | Grand, old, greedy, condescending, dryly funny. Speaks of "my kiln", "my pots", "our fire". Calls you "little thief", "delver", "dear" (Aunt Ebb). Never explains the plot. | 12 words or fewer |
| **The Hearthkeeper** | Wick's one voice (about 30 lines that react to progress) | Dry, warm, practical, few words. Says what matters and stops. Never cheers. | 15 words or fewer |
| **The game** | Tips, buttons, tooltips, loss and win screens, store text | Plain, second person, imperative. Tells you what to do, with exact numbers. Never in character. | Tips 20 words or fewer; buttons 1 or 2 words |

### Rules for every line

1. **Concrete over grand.** Name a thing you could hold or see: a pot, a coal, a ladle, a raft. "Mind the pots" beats "Beware the ancient guardians".
2. **Short.** Inside the lengths above. One idea per line. No line needs a second sentence to make sense.
3. **Funny by being sure of itself, never by joking.** The Court is funny because it takes itself seriously. No puns on purpose, no winks at the player, no pop-culture lines, no "lol"-style UI ("Oops!").
4. **Dark, never grim.** Cold, hunger and loss are in the world; gore, despair, death speeches and cruelty to Wick are not.
5. **The game voice is never in character.** A tip says "Quench the blue cast." It never says "The ancient flame stirs; heed its call."
6. **Exact numbers in the game voice.** "Take 60% less damage for 6 s." Not "greatly reduces damage".
7. **US spelling in player text** (armor, color). Player text already uses "armor" (sim/content/classes.ts:65); code comments mix both, which doesn't matter. The slop sweep checks player text.
8. **No exclamation marks** in the Court's or Hearthkeeper's voice, and at most one in the whole game voice per screen.
9. **Sentence case** for buttons and headings ("Delve again", not "DELVE AGAIN" or "Delve Again"), except proper names.
10. **No em-dashes or ellipses for drama.** Use a full stop.

### Banned words (they read as generated)

Never in any player-facing name or line. The list is enforced by one editor (and, once Checkpoint 2 has text tables, by a unit test over sim/content).

- **From the vision (rule 4):** ancient, eldritch, arcane, void, titan, shadow- (as a prefix or compound), -heart (as a suffix), -breaker, -bane, doom-.
- **Added here:** abyss, abyssal, celestial, infernal, primal, primordial, ethereal, mystic, mystical, nexus, essence, realm, destiny, chosen, forsaken, cursed, unholy, soul-, blood- (as prefix), -born, -forged, -sworn, everlasting, eternal, legend/legendary and epic (except as the Steve-picked rarity names), unleash, embark, journey, adventure (in store or UI text), "rise of", "dawn of", "age of".
- **Shapes, not words:** made-up syllables (Nhevra, Ysmeret), apostrophes for flavor (Ka'zul), fake Latin, "-ia" or "-or" endings on invented words, double vowels for exotic feel (Aaeron), and any "Adjective-noun of the Noun" chain ("Blade of the Endless Night").
- **Fire-word budget:** *ember* means the currency and stolen fire. No new name uses Ember-, Cinder-, Ash- or Smolder- as a prefix. Today's content leans on them hard (grep over sim/content names: Slag 31, Cinder 21, Ember 16, Ashbuck 9), and that repetition is itself a "generated" tell.

### Sample lines (drafts for Steve, not final)

These set the voice. Steve approves the first batch of about 50 before more are written (vision, risks).

**The Kiln Mother**
- Back again, little thief? You still smell of my kiln.
- Mind the pots. I fired every one of them.
- *(you fall)* Into the kiln with you. Everything comes out harder.
- *(she falls)* Take it, then. I can always make more than you can carry.

**Dowager Bellows**
- Wipe your feet. Oh, what's the point now.
- *(her Temper builds)* Do you know what the damp does to my bellows?

**The Glass Heir**
- Hold still. I want to see what all the fuss is about.
- *(you hide behind a pillar)* I can wait. I'm very good at looking.

**The Banked Queen**
- Shut the door. You're letting the warm in.
- *(your coal goes out)* There. Isn't that better.

**Aunt Ebb**
- Everything that floats down here is mine, dear. That includes you.

**Uncle Glut**
- Is that for me? It's always for me.

**The Furnace Regent**
- By order of the Regent, acting for Grandmother, who is asleep: burn.

**Grandmother Slag**
- I hoarded fire before your town had a name.

**The Hearthkeeper**
- *(first launch)* The Court took the fire. Go down and take some back.
- *(after the Quench lesson)* It fears water. Of course it does.
- *(first ember home)* Bring more.
- *(after a loss)* You came back. That's the part that counts. The embers help.
- *(the Forge lights)* The smith says the Forge is warm enough to work. Don't tell her I said thank you.
- *(idle in town)* Sit a minute. The fire's not going anywhere. That's the trouble.
