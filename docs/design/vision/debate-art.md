# Art Director debate: where I stand after reading everyone

*27 Nov 2026. **(judgment)** marks my opinion.*

## Where we agree

All five proposals found the same faults in the same screenshots, so I treat these as settled:
- **Scale.** Combat and I set heroes at 70 to 80 px. Product's 60 px minimum is too small: the market report says small things "read as cheap and hard".
- **One look:** low-poly with outlines, and an end to the 2D/3D mix (research-market recipe point 4).
- **Signal colours.** Combat's red for move, gold for stand here and blue for interrupt match my reserved Signal set. We arrived at this separately.
- **Suno goes before we charge.** 85% of players feel negative about generative AI, music is among the most disliked uses (Quantic Foundry), and Steam would require a disclosure.
- **Handwritten names, a smaller launch, and a one-room slice first.**

## Where I disagree

**1. The research leans toward pixel art (P10). I still choose low-poly.** P10 says "hand-made pixel art is safer than a richer look made *with AI*." It compares hand-made with AI-made, not pixels with polygons. The market report studied looks and names outlined low-poly the "most realistic" style for agents (Thronefall). Pixel art at 70 to 80 px means redrawing all 5,000 lines of textures.ts at about 2.5 times the size. Low-poly gives us the shatter, the lighting and rendered icons from one kit. Pixel art is still the fallback if checkpoint 1 fails, so the blind side-by-side test decides, not my preference.

**2. Product's store line, "every sprite and sound hand-built."** It is only true once Suno is gone, and "sprite" is wrong for a 3D game. I'd write "No generative AI art or music." With almost half of new Steam releases now disclosing AI use, saying we use none is the stronger claim.

**3. Eight launch places (Systems) costs too much.** Each place needs a floor, a landmark, its foes and a boss, about a week of art. Eight places instead of six adds two weeks of art plus its review.

**4. The relit town (Creative) needs limits.** Art scope: one fixed diorama, with each building drawn cold or lit, and no walking or camera moves. Buildings light at milestones, never by spending embers (Systems).

**5. Damage numbers (Combat)** must stay small, never use a Signal colour, and never hide a telegraph. My rule holds: **if it glows flat, it matters.**

## What I now adopt

- **Combat's eight foe jobs become my foe art brief.** Each job (Charger, Spitter, Shielder, Caster, Mender, Bomber, Swarmling, Brute) gets one base silhouette, re-glazed for each place, plus one twist foe per place. That replaces about 25 one-off foes with 8 bodies and about 7 twists, saves roughly a week, and lets a foe's job read from its outline.
- **Packs of 3 to 5 foes** also pay for the outlines: fewer draw calls on a mid-range Android phone.
- **One shared slice room.** Combat's hit flash, hit-stop, haptics and flick-dodge, plus my shatter and material sounds, are one task, not two.
- **Product's screenshot gate:** "if no screenshot sells, the game isn't ready."
- **Product's CC0 fallback.** If our in-code music loses Steve's blind test, use human-made CC0 loops in the closed test while we get composer quotes.
- **The mastery end screen** (Systems, Combat, Product), with the killing blow shown as a small diorama.

## What I think is wrong or too costly

- **Keeping the 2D look as an option.** It doubles every art task for good. A low-power 3D mode is the fallback. Steve must approve this, because it reverses his earlier call.
- **A unique model for each of about 60 Heirlooms.** Icons come from the slot kit, and only about 10 standout pieces get their own model.
- **Redoing the art across the game before the loop is proven.** I agree with Systems here: the art scales only after the one-room gate.

## Three decisions for the team

1. **The hand-made bet.** A full diorama redo, no 2D mode, and in-code music, with CC0 and then a composer as fallbacks. Each part is gated by one Kiln room that Steve judges blind against today's build.
2. **Launch scope: 6, 7 or 8 places**, at about one week of art each. And does the Hoard raid ship with bots?
3. **The name.** Keep "Horders" or rename it (Emberthief, Kilnward, Hearthless)? The title screen, app icon and store art depend on it, so we decide before checkpoint 2.

## My vote

**Hook:** *Steal fire from a furnace full of greedy kings: real boss fights you read and beat in portrait, ten minutes at a time.*

**Core loop: keep, and adjust.** Keep the delves, the stick, the four buttons, the automatic basic attack and the telegraphs. Adjust:
- a closer camera
- packs of 3 to 5 foes built from jobs
- the flick-dodge
- a visible enrage in place of Burnout
- Sparks plus one Relic per run
- losses that always pay

**Art direction:** kiln-lit low-poly dioramas, with a cracked clay Court against cast-iron heroes in black, lit only by fire. Only gameplay signals get flat, bright colour.

**Top 5 priorities:**
1. **One Kiln room, final in look and feel:** the camera, the feel kit, the flick-dodge, the shatter, the material sounds and the ember motif. Steve judges it blind.
2. **The hand-made pass before any price:** replace Suno, write the world bible, settle the name, and hand-write the item names.
3. **A first 15 minutes that sells the game:** the title screen, a tutorial that ends at the Kiln Mother, a one-button hub, and the mastery end screen.
4. **A loop that replays:** Sparks and Fusions, Relics, time budgets for every fight, and a melee answer to every boss attack.
5. **Spread the look across the cut scope:** the job-based foe kit, a landmark for each place, and rendered hub cards and icons, with five cold testers at each gate.
