# What makes action roguelites and dungeon games last for years

*Research for Horders, 27 Nov 2026. It answers Steve's questions: what makes highly rated, long-lasting games stick, where their replay value comes from, and what the hook is.*

**How to read this.** Claims about other games or the market link to a source. Where a point is my own reading, it says **(judgment)**. Where it comes from my general knowledge of how a game plays, not from a source I fetched today, it says **(play knowledge)**. One game here (the timed-key dungeon mode from a big MMO) is studied only to learn the pattern. No names or content from it, or from Azeroth Survivors, appear in any proposal.

---

## 1. The short answer

The games that last share five traits. The rest of this report explains each one.

1. **A run is short and every run pays out.** Vampire Survivors runs top out at 15 to 30 minutes ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vampire_Survivors)). Marvel Snap matches last about 3 minutes ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/ben-brode-bets-super-speed-will-make-marvel-snap-stand-out/)). Hades set out "to take the pain out of dying" ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-supergiant-weaves-narrative-rewards-into-i-hades-i-cycle-of-perpetual-death)). Losing still moves you forward.
2. **Builds you assemble during the run, from parts that combine in surprising ways.** Balatro's jokers, Risk of Rain 2's stacking items and Slay the Spire's cards all work this way. The player feels clever, and two runs never play alike.
3. **One idea anyone gets in seconds, with depth that takes hundreds of hours to find.** Balatro is "a remix and retune of poker" ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/exploring-balatros-hype-its-ingenious-twists-on-poker-and-its-mysterious-creator)). Marvel Snap aimed for "a really approachable, super fast game, that has incredible depth" ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/ben-brode-bets-super-speed-will-make-marvel-snap-stand-out/)).
4. **A long ladder of harder rules.** Slay the Spire has 20 Ascension levels ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slay_the_Spire)). Hades has Heat, Balatro has stakes, and the timed-key dungeon mode has key levels with weekly modifiers. Each step changes how you play, not only the numbers.
5. **Years of steady, generous updates, and respect for the player's wallet and time.** Dead Cells shipped 35 updates and 5 DLCs over 7 years ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/games/roguelike/dead-cells-takes-a-bow-with-its-final-major-update-calling-an-end-to-7-years-of-development-that-spanned-35-updates-5-dlc-and-2-studios)). Path of Exile ships a new league on a "rigid 13-week schedule" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/the-making-of-path-of-exile)). The lowest ratings in this study went to games that sold power: Diablo Immortal holds the lowest user score in Metacritic history ([VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/diablo-immortal-now-has-the-lowest-user-score-in-metacritic-history)).

**What it means for Horders (judgment).** Horders already has most of the systems the genre uses: drafts, tiers, Omens, keys, dailies, weeklies, seasons and co-op. What it lacks is a feeling of one place to be, a first five minutes that sells it, runs that feel powerful and juicy, and losses that still pay. The top games are not the ones with the most systems. They are the ones where every system serves one fantasy you can say in one sentence. Section 5 rates Horders against each principle.

## 3. Case studies

Each case covers the hook (first five minutes), the core loop, where replay value comes from, the retention cadence, and what reviewers praise and punish.

### 3.1 Vampire Survivors (poncle)
- **Hook:** You press start and are playing in seconds. The only control is movement (no attack button), one weapon that fires on its own, and the first level-up comes in about a minute. Within five minutes the screen is full of enemies and your power is visibly climbing. (Play knowledge.)
- **Core loop:** Move, collect gems, level up, pick a weapon or passive from a few choices, open chests. Most weapons have an evolved form ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vampire_Survivors)). After 15 to 30 minutes the Reaper arrives and the run ends.
- **Replay sources:** A long achievement list "encourages the use of different characters or gameplay strategies," and there are 49 characters ([The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/vampire-survivors-how-developers-used-gambling-psychology-to-create-a-bafta-winning-game-203613)). Secret unlocks, evolution recipes, and gold spent on permanent upgrades so "no run ever feels wasted" (same source).
- **Cadence:** Short sessions. Retention comes from steady free updates and paid DLC. It won BAFTA's Evolving Game award in 2025 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vampire_Survivors)).
- **Praise:** Price against hours. The creator talks about getting "an afternoon of entertainment" for the price of a movie ticket, and expected nobody to play more than two hours. He has about 3,000 himself ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/roguelike/on-steam-alone-i-probably-have-3-000-hours-nobody-loves-vampire-survivors-more-than-its-creator-who-once-thought-nobody-would-play-it-for-more-than-two-hours-and-ironically-says-short-games-rule)). Rave scores (Metacritic 86 to 95 across platforms).
- **Punished:** Simple-looking art, and a near-gambling reward loop that some writers question (The Conversation). Neither stopped it.
- **Lesson:** Fast power growth on screen, plus a huge list of things to unlock, beats polish. The art is cheap but consistent, with one gothic look.

### 3.2 Hades and Hades II (Supergiant)
- **Hook:** Strong art and voice from the first second. A named hero with a clear goal (escape the underworld). The first room teaches the dash, and the first god's gift (a "boon") arrives within minutes. (Play knowledge.)
- **Core loop:** Fight through rooms, pick one of three rewards at each door, take boons from gods, beat the area boss, die or win, return home, spend currencies, talk to characters, go again.
- **Replay sources:** The story moves forward through deaths. The game reads the state of your run and picks fitting scenes, "to have those moments where you feel the game is paying attention" ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-supergiant-weaves-narrative-rewards-into-i-hades-i-cycle-of-perpetual-death)). Boon combinations, six weapons with variants, and the Heat difficulty system. God Mode adds a little damage resistance every time you die, so anyone can finish ([Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/how-hades-made-a-genre-known-for-being-impossibly-hard-accessible)).
- **Cadence:** Run-based. No dailies. It kept players through story and mastery alone.
- **Praise:** "Death isn't a fail state" (Vice). Hades II (PC Gamer 88) has "flexible combat and unforgettable characters" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/action/hades-2-review)).
- **Punished:** Hades II's opening hours are "tedious," with "every major feature … locked behind a recipe," and it is "fussier than the elegantly simple original" (PC Gamer).
- **Lesson:** A world and characters turn losing into progress. Too many systems gated early hurts even the best studio.

### 3.3 Slay the Spire (Mega Crit)
- **Hook:** One clear screen. A map you choose a path on, a simple first fight where you can see what the enemy will do next turn. (Play knowledge.)
- **Core loop:** Pick a route on the map, fight, add one of three cards to your deck, collect relics, beat three acts.
- **Replay sources:** Four characters with their own decks, 20 Ascension levels that each add a lasting penalty, and a daily climb with one fixed seed for everyone ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slay_the_Spire)).
- **Cadence:** Daily climb, plus the Ascension ladder for years.
- **How it got balanced:** The team logged every choice. It tracked how often each card was picked when offered and how often it sat in winning decks, and grew its dashboards from 3 graphs to 90. Weekly patches during Early Access ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-i-slay-the-spire-i-s-devs-use-data-to-balance-their-roguelike-deck-builder)). Single-player let them "prioritize fun over strict balance" (same source).
- **Praise:** Metacritic 89, 98% of critics recommend it, PC Gamer's Best Design award for 2019 (Wikipedia).
- **Punished:** Plain art. The mobile port was later stuck without updates when its publisher shut down (Wikipedia).
- **Lesson:** Show the enemy's intent, let the player plan, measure every choice and fix the dead ones.

### 3.4 Balatro (LocalThunk)
- **Hook:** You already know poker hands. Play a hand, watch the score count up with juicy sound and shake, and pass the first blind in a minute. (Play knowledge.)
- **Core loop:** Play poker hands to beat a score target (a "blind"), earn money, buy jokers and upgrades in a shop, beat the boss blind that changes the rules, repeat across eight antes.
- **Replay sources:** 150 jokers ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/card-games/balatro-was-only-supposed-to-have-120-jokers-but-instead-of-correcting-a-publisher-mistake-localthunk-just-made-30-more-of-them/)), decks and stakes that make it harder, and boss blinds. "Numbers getting bigger" is the core conflict ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/exploring-balatros-hype-its-ingenious-twists-on-poker-and-its-mysterious-creator)).
- **Cadence:** None beyond runs and unlocks. No ads, no microtransactions, no battle pass ([Gfinity](https://www.gfinityesports.com/article/save-your-wallet-and-time-with-this-goty-contender-that-has-no-microtransactions-or-battle-passes)).
- **Praise:** Simplicity. The creator avoided gamer jargon because it adds "so many layers of complexity," and wanted no hit points, "almost like solitaire" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/i-dont-play-poker-at-all-says-solo-developer-who-made-the-poker-roguelike-i-cant-stop-playing/)). 90 on Metacritic.
- **Punished on mobile:** No portrait mode, and touch "track[s] your finger as if it was a mouse cursor" ([148Apps](https://148apps.com/balatro/review)).
- **Lesson:** A solo developer with simple art sold 5 million copies by making one idea feel great and stay readable. Portrait-first is a real advantage on phones, and reviewers notice when it is missing.

### 3.5 Brotato (Blobfish)
- **Hook:** Pick a potato with an odd rule (for example, one that can only hold one weapon), and wave 1 starts in seconds. (Play knowledge.)
- **Core loop:** Survive a short wave in a small arena, then shop between waves for weapons and items, about 20 waves to a win.
- **Replay sources:** Characters that are "surprisingly unique to use" ([TouchArcade](https://toucharcade.com/2023/04/04/brotato-iphone-ipad-android-review/)). Each one changes the rules, not just the stats, plus danger levels.
- **Cadence:** Runs "just long enough for a good hunker" on mobile (TouchArcade).
- **Praise:** Users rate it "overwhelmingly positive," and it sold over 10 million copies by 2025 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brotato)).
- **Punished:** A smaller meta than Vampire Survivors (TouchArcade), and death "a bit too easily" (Rock Paper Shotgun, via Wikipedia). Metacritic 76.
- **Lesson:** Characters with one rule-bending twist each give cheap, huge replay. A shop between waves gives a calm planning beat between action beats.

### 3.6 Dead Cells (Motion Twin, later Evil Empire)
- **Hook:** Fluid movement and hard-hitting melee from the first room. (Play knowledge.)
- **Core loop:** Fight through linked biomes, find weapons, spend cells between biomes on permanent upgrades and item blueprints. You lose unspent cells when you die, but the upgrades stay ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/dead-cells-review)).
- **Replay sources:** Combat mastery, branching routes, and levels that "rearrange after each death but stay recognizable" (same review). Boss Cells raise the difficulty.
- **Cadence:** Years of free updates and DLC. It was called "a perfect example of Early Access done right" ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/dead-cells-is-a-perfect-example-of-early-access-done-right)). Over 10 million sold ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/dead-cells-has-topped-10-million-sales-worldwide)).
- **Praise:** 90/100: "superbly-executed" movement and combat, with each biome adding "a new splash of colour" (PC Gamer review).
- **Punished:** Going back to the start makes boss practice "a slog." The weapon you need may not spawn. "It can be pretty mean sometimes" (same review).
- **Lesson:** How it feels in the hand is the base everything else stands on. Biomes that each look different make the journey feel like a place.

### 3.7 Risk of Rain 2 (Hopoo)
- **Hook:** You drop onto a big alien map, open chests and get your first items within a minute. (Play knowledge.)
- **Core loop:** Explore, open chests for items, find the teleporter, survive the boss event, move to the next stage. Difficulty rises with the clock, so speed matters.
- **Replay sources:** Items that stack without limit. The game "knows that all the fun is in that exploitation" ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/risk-of-rain-2-review)). Survivors with their own kits, hidden stages and logs.
- **Cadence:** Co-op nights with friends and DLC.
- **Praise:** Feeling unstoppable, and co-op splitting up and regrouping for bosses (PC Gamer, 84).
- **Punished:** Solo "dilutes" the fun, levels feel sparse, the visuals are simple (same review).
- **Lesson:** Let players break the game in a single-player run. A clock that pushes difficulty up keeps the pace honest.

### 3.8 Diablo III and Diablo IV seasons (case for the season model)
- **Model:** Each season starts a fresh character with a unique rule change, with cosmetic rewards and leaderboard placement. Seasons kept Diablo III going for about nine years ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/pour-one-out-for-diablo-3-as-blizzard-prepares-its-final-new-season)).
- **What went wrong in Diablo IV Season 1:** It launched while casual players were still on the campaign. A patch made "across-the-board nerfs." The seasonal power felt weak. There was no leaderboard ("I'd rather have a fully-fledged board than a half-baked attempt at a season"). Battle pass rewards were dull. Cosmetics were rarely seen ([Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/gaming/diablo-4-having-its-first-season-so-soon-after-launch-was-a-mistake)). The developers said in a stream: "we know it's not fun" ([Push Square](https://www.pushsquare.com/news/2023/07/diablo-4-devs-front-irate-fanbase-in-frank-campfire-chat-we-know-its-bad-we-know-its-not-fun)).
- **Lesson:** A season works when it adds a new *power* toy and a reason to show off. Taking power away (nerfs) at the start of a season is the fastest way to lose players.

### 3.9 Path of Exile (Grinding Gear Games)
- **Model:** A deep free action RPG with a huge build space. A new league every 13 weeks brings a new mechanic and a fresh economy. Each league lifts player numbers by about 20% ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/the-making-of-path-of-exile)).
- **Money:** "Ethical F2P": cosmetics, supporter packs and stash tabs. "Nothing purchasable makes the game more enjoyable" in the gameplay sense ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/path-of-exile-2-is-sticking-to-its-ethical-f2p-live-service-model-instead-of-chasing-diablo-4s-success)).
- **Praise:** Depth and fair pricing.
- **Punished:** Complexity puts off new players (judgment, widely said). Stash tabs feel close to required for serious play (see the [Steam discussions](https://steamcommunity.com/app/238960/discussions/0/3482997257340816247)).
- **Lesson:** A predictable update rhythm that players can plan around is a retention engine of its own.

### 3.10 Timed-key dungeons (an MMO's five-player endgame mode; studied for the pattern only)
- **Model:** A key names a dungeon and level. Beat the par time (about 30 minutes) and the key goes up by 1 to 3 levels. Go over time and it drops one. Deaths cost time. Modifiers rotate weekly. A season score sums your best timed run in each dungeon. A weekly vault gives 1, 2 or 3 reward choices for doing 1, 4 or 8 runs ([Gfinity](https://www.gfinityesports.com/article/wow-mythic-explained-keystones-affixes-and-how-dungeon-score-works)).
- **Why it sticks:** The same dungeons stay fresh because the modifiers and the clock change how you route and fight. The weekly vault rewards showing up, not grinding. The score shows your mastery to others.
- **Punished:** Pressure and toxicity in groups formed by strangers, and keys "both a blessing and a curse" ([Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/mythic-is-both-a-blessing-and-a-curse-in-wow-the-war-withins-endgame/)).
- **Lesson for Horders (judgment):** The pattern (a par time, a climbing key, a modifier of the week, a capped weekly reward chest) is a generic idea. Horders' Delver's Keys and Omens already point this way. We should build it in our own words, never this game's.

### 3.11 Archero and Archero 2 (Habby)
- **Hook:** One stick. You move to dodge, and your hero shoots automatically at the nearest enemy ([Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2019/8/9/why-archero-banked-25m-but-leaves-25m-hanging-hlx9n)). In Archero 2, standing still triggers the attacks. The first session opens with a reward wheel ([Tiny Teardown](https://someselectedstories.substack.com/p/tiny-teardown-archero-2-habby-games)).
- **Core loop:** Clear short rooms, pick 1 of 3 abilities from about 50 on each level-up, meet angel and devil offers, fight a boss (Deconstructor of Fun).
- **Replay sources:** Ability combinations, chapters, a six-slot gear meta, and talents.
- **Cadence:** Features unlock "over multiple days of sessions, not your usual 20 minute tutorial which unlocks the whole UI" ([MobileGamer](https://mobilegamer.biz/the-design-secrets-powering-habbys-hit-machine/)). Daily quests, 28-day battle passes, weekly arenas, twice-daily boss events (Tiny Teardown).
- **Praise:** Easy to pick up, satisfying one-thumb play.
- **Punished:** Progress slows for mid- and late-game players. Talents fade in impact. New drops feel worthless next to high-tier gear. Chests lose value (Deconstructor of Fun). Archero 2 adds confusing gates and a "very linear" pass (Tiny Teardown).
- **Lesson:** Unlock systems one at a time over days. And loot has to stay exciting at hour 50, not only at hour 1.

### 3.12 Soul Knight (ChillyRoom)
- **Hook:** A pixel dungeon shooter with auto-aim and twin-stick-style touch controls. You are shooting within seconds. (Play knowledge.)
- **Core loop:** Clear randomly generated rooms across floors, grab weapons (400+), beat bosses.
- **Replay sources:** 20+ heroes, weapons, offline LAN and online co-op ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/soul-knight/id1184159988)).
- **Praise:** 4.8 stars from about 127k ratings. "Easy controls," frequent updates, no ads after levels, offline play, and a roster you can unlock without paying (App Store reviews).
- **Punished:** Characters that feel pay-to-win, and achievements that need paid heroes (App Store reviews).
- **Lesson:** On phones, players reward games with no forced ads that work offline and are fair to free players. One cohesive pixel style carried a huge content list for years.

### 3.13 Marvel Snap and Hearthstone (session design)
- **Marvel Snap:** About 3 minutes a match, never more than 5, with simultaneous turns: "double the speed, but not half of the decisions." 12-card decks. The "snap" doubles the stakes and adds bluffing ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/ben-brode-bets-super-speed-will-make-marvel-snap-stand-out/)).
- **Hearthstone:** When it made quests slower to reward committed players, the change had "the exact opposite effect." Casual players played less, and committed players found weeklies "a huge chore." Blizzard reverted them ([MMOBomb](https://mmobomb.com/news/progression-changes-aimed-getting-players-to-play-more-did-exact-opposite-so-hearthstone-new-progression-updates-inbound)).
- **Lesson:** Fit a session into a phone's "waiting in line" moment. Dailies and weeklies must feel like gifts, not chores.

### 3.14 Market reference: mobile retention benchmarks
- In 2024 the top 25% of mobile games kept about 26–28% of players on day 1 and 7–8% on day 7. Three-quarters of games keep under 3% by day 28. Median sessions last 5–6 minutes (8–9 in the top 25%). Midcore games average 6–7 sessions a day ([GameAnalytics via GameDevReports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-mobile-gaming-benchmarks)).
- **What it means (judgment):** An 8 to 12 minute delve is longer than a typical phone session. A complete short mode (a 3 to 5 minute "one fight" option) would match how phones are used. The daily bounty may already be that.
- **The "AI slop" risk is measurable.** A 2026 analysis of 9,879 Steam games released in 2025 found that disclosing AI use went with about a 53% drop in review counts, and the effect hit high-potential games hardest ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/software/ai/data-analyst-finds-ai-stigma-on-steam-can-reduce-the-number-of-reviews-a-game-gets-by-around-53-percent-and-the-reviews-it-does-get-are-more-negative/)). Developers even get accused of AI use with no evidence ([Creative Bloq](https://creativebloq.com/ai/ai-slop-has-become-a-harmful-insult-hurled-around-with-no-evidence-game-developers-claim)). Steve's worry is backed by data.

## 4. Fourteen principles that cut across these games

Each principle names the games that show it and what it could mean for Horders (judgment).

**P1. Playing within 10 seconds, power within 60.** Vampire Survivors, Brotato, Archero and Soul Knight all drop you into play almost at once and give the first upgrade within a minute. Balatro's first scored hand counts up with sound in under a minute.
*Horders:* the first launch goes to a busy delve picker with 8 chips (13-01-hub.jpg). A brand-new player should land in the tutorial Kiln with one tap and get their first draft card inside a minute.

**P2. One sentence of fantasy, and everything serves it.** "Escape the underworld" (Hades). "Poker, but you break it" (Balatro). "Survive the night" (Vampire Survivors). Each system serves that sentence.
*Horders:* the audit found no world, and names grew one feature at a time. We need one sentence, such as "You are a delver who goes down into the burning Hoard to take back what it swallowed," then rename and cut systems to fit it.

**P3. Losing still pays.** Hades's story moves on through deaths ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-supergiant-weaves-narrative-rewards-into-i-hades-i-cycle-of-perpetual-death)). Vampire Survivors and Dead Cells keep currency and unlocks. Hades's God Mode makes every death a little easier.
*Horders:* the loss screen in 13-13-end-full.jpg shows "No loot this time" and half the screen empty. A loss should always give something visible: progress toward an unlock, a bestiary entry, a line of story, and one clear lesson.

**P4. Builds come from parts that combine in surprising ways.** Balatro's 150 jokers, Risk of Rain 2's stacking items, Hades boons with duo boons, and Vampire Survivors evolutions. The thrill comes from the combination, not from +5%.
*Horders:* the draft cards (13-09-draft.jpg) are clean, but they read as flat stat changes ("costs 6 less Heat"). Add a few cards that *change behaviour* and pair across branches, such as "Blink leaves a flame trail" paired with "standing in your own fire gives Heat."

**P5. Let players break it.** Risk of Rain 2 "knows that all the fun is in that exploitation" ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/risk-of-rain-2-review)). Slay the Spire puts fun ahead of strict balance in single player ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-i-slay-the-spire-i-s-devs-use-data-to-balance-their-roguelike-deck-builder)).
*Horders:* solo delves should allow a rare broken run. Balance tightly only what co-op and the leaderboards need.

**P6. Difficulty as a ladder of rules.** Slay the Spire's 20 Ascensions, Hades's Heat, Balatro's stakes, and timed keys with weekly modifiers. Each step asks for a new habit.
*Horders:* Horders has tiers (×1.22 HP, ×1.12 damage per tier) and Omens from tier 3. Lean on Omens and less on raw numbers. Bigger numbers alone are what made Diablo IV's nerfed season feel bad.

**P7. Mastery you can see and show.** Dead Cells' combat, Slay the Spire's showing enemy intent, a timed-key season score, and leaderboards. Players stay when they can tell they are getting better.
*Horders:* Horders' telegraphs (red to dodge, gold to stand in, cast bars to interrupt) are already a mastery language. Show it after each fight: "You dodged 9 of 11 slams, interrupted 3 of 4 casts." A personal best per dungeon gives a reason to replay.

**P8. Characters that change the rules, not just the stats.** Brotato's potatoes, Hades's weapons, and Slay the Spire's four decks.
*Horders:* five classes is the right number. Each needs one verb nobody else has that changes how you move. Positioning already says this ("Each class gets one verb no one else has"). The audit says melee classes fall apart in later solo dungeons, which has to be fixed first.

**P9. Juice is not optional.** Vlambeer's "Art of Screenshake" talk showed that 30 small tweaks turn a dull shooter into a satisfying one ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/vlambeer-co-founder-shares-advice-on-building-better-action-games)). Dead Cells' review credits "animation, pixel art, and sound design" for how combat feels ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/dead-cells-review)). Balatro's score count-up is a big part of its pull (play knowledge).
*Horders:* the audit says hits have little weight and enemies are a few pixels tall (13-08-hud.jpg). A dedicated feel pass is likely the best value for effort on the list: bigger enemies, hit flash, knockback, hit-stop, damage numbers, and death bursts.

**P10. One cohesive look beats a fancy look.** Vampire Survivors, Slay the Spire, Brotato and Soul Knight all sold millions with simple but consistent art. Reviewers mark down "simple visuals" (Risk of Rain 2) but forgive them when the style is consistent.
*Horders:* the room sheet (rooms-sheet-0.png) shows the same room in five colour washes, with small 2D heroes over 3D floors. Pick one look and apply it everywhere, before adding any more content. Given the AI-stigma data, hand-made, consistent pixel art is safer than a richer look made with AI.

**P11. Short sessions that end cleanly.** Marvel Snap takes 3 minutes, Brotato is "just long enough," and the median phone session is 5–6 minutes ([GameAnalytics](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-mobile-gaming-benchmarks)).
*Horders:* 8 to 12 minutes per delve is fine for a "proper" session, but offer a 3 to 5 minute option (one fight, the daily bounty) and let a delve be paused and resumed.

**P12. A calendar players can plan around, made of gifts and not chores.** Path of Exile's 13-week leagues, Diablo III's seasons, Slay the Spire's daily climb, and the weekly vault's 1/4/8 runs. Hearthstone shows the flip side: slower quests made casual players play less.
*Horders:* Horders has a daily bounty, weekly quests and 6-week seasons. Keep them, but cap them and make them light (for example, 3 weekly tasks that complete through normal play). Show one "today" card in the hub, not eight chips.

**P13. Unlock systems one at a time.** Habby unlocks features over days ([MobileGamer](https://mobilegamer.biz/the-design-secrets-powering-habbys-hit-machine/)). Hades II was punished for gating too much behind recipes early ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/action/hades-2-review)).
*Horders:* by tier 6 the hub shows Bounty, Key, Weekly, Seen, Season, Omens, Potion and Vent Floor. Hide each until it matters, and introduce each with one line and one reward.

**P14. Fair money and a fair price.** Path of Exile's "ethical F2P," Balatro's no-microtransactions stance, Soul Knight reviewers praising no ads after levels, Vampire Survivors' movie-ticket pricing. Diablo Immortal's pay-for-power got the lowest user score in Metacritic history ([VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/diablo-immortal-now-has-the-lowest-user-score-in-metacritic-history)).
*Horders:* the safest choices for ratings are a low one-time price ($1 to $5) or free with cosmetic-only purchases. Never sell power. If we use ads, make them rewarded and optional only.

**P15 (bonus). Measure every choice and fix the dead ones.** Slay the Spire logged pick rates and win rates and patched weekly ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-i-slay-the-spire-i-s-devs-use-data-to-balance-their-roguelike-deck-builder)).
*Horders:* Horders has bot playtests (average.md). Add pick rate and win rate per draft card and per ability option, since the audit shows Bulwark's Muster at 24/40 against Anvil Skin at 6/40.

## 5. Horders against the principles (judgment, from the audit and screenshots)

| Principle | Today | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| P1 Fast hook | Tutorial Kiln exists and is hard to lose | No title or "who am I" moment; first screen is a busy picker |
| P2 One fantasy | Strong "forge and fire" mood in the Kiln | No world; names and themes drift; two different systems both called "talents" |
| P3 Losing pays | XP, world boss damage and achievements still count | "No loot this time"; half-empty loss screen; one-line lesson |
| P4 Combining builds | 3-card drafts, trees, verb sets | Cards read as stat tweaks; few combinations that surprise |
| P5 Allowed to break | Unknown | Not a stated design goal |
| P6 Ladder of rules | Tiers 1–15, Omens, Delver's Keys | Tiers lean on numbers; 6 Omens is thin for years of play |
| P7 Visible mastery | Telegraphs, cast bars, interrupts | Nothing shows your skill after a fight; no personal bests |
| P8 Rule-changing classes | 5 classes with resources (Heat and others) | Melee fails at average skill late; one sprite per class |
| P9 Juice | Some screen shake, one hit-stop | No feel pass; tiny enemies; weak hits |
| P10 Cohesive look | Clean UI kit | 2D/3D mix, single colour wash per dungeon, no art on hub cards |
| P11 Short sessions | 8–12 min delve, daily bounty | No 3–5 min "one fight" mode stated as such |
| P12 Gift calendar | Daily, weekly, 6-week season, world boss | Shown as 8 separate chips; reads as chores |
| P13 Gradual unlocks | Some gating by tier | Too many chips visible at once by tier 6 |
| P14 Fair money | Not decided | Decide before launch; stay away from selling power |

**The two Horders strengths no case study has together (judgment):** portrait-first touch play, which even Balatro's mobile port lacks ([148Apps](https://148apps.com/balatro/review)), and boss fights you read and react to, in a phone game with roles. No game above combines them. That combination is the hook to sell.

## 6. Anti-patterns that kill retention or ratings

1. **Selling power.** Diablo Immortal: lowest user score in Metacritic history ([VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/diablo-immortal-now-has-the-lowest-user-score-in-metacritic-history)). Soul Knight's few complaints are about paid characters ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/soul-knight/id1184159988)).
2. **Taking fun away at the start of a season.** Diablo IV Season 1's "across-the-board nerfs" ([Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/gaming/diablo-4-having-its-first-season-so-soon-after-launch-was-a-mistake)).
3. **Launching a season or live system before it is ready.** Same source: "I'd rather have a fully-fledged board than a half-baked attempt at a season."
4. **Chores dressed as quests.** Hearthstone's slower quests made casual players play less ([MMOBomb](https://mmobomb.com/news/progression-changes-aimed-getting-players-to-play-more-did-exact-opposite-so-hearthstone-new-progression-updates-inbound)).
5. **Front-loading systems and gates.** Hades II's "tedious" opening ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/action/hades-2-review)) and Archero 2's confusing gates ([Tiny Teardown](https://someselectedstories.substack.com/p/tiny-teardown-archero-2-habby-games)).
6. **Loot that stops mattering.** Archero's mid-game drops feel insignificant and its chests lose value ([Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2019/8/9/why-archero-banked-25m-but-leaves-25m-hanging-hlx9n)).
7. **Losses that waste your time.** Dead Cells' boss practice is a "slog" after a reset ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/dead-cells-review)). Kasavin: players must "feel the time you spent wasn't a waste."
8. **Anything that reads as AI-made, or as a generic name list.** It went with about 53% fewer reviews in a study of 2025 Steam games ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/software/ai/data-analyst-finds-ai-stigma-on-steam-can-reduce-the-number-of-reviews-a-game-gets-by-around-53-percent-and-the-reviews-it-does-get-are-more-negative/)). Horders' "prefix + slot" gear names (items.ts) are this pattern.
9. **Ignoring the phone.** Balatro's mobile port lost points for having no portrait mode and for mouse-like touch ([148Apps](https://148apps.com/balatro/review)). Forced ads drive complaints too (judgment, based on the Soul Knight praise for having none).
10. **Fights that run too long.** (Judgment, backed by our own data.) A boss lost to "Burnout (the fight ran too long)" after 6:08 (13-13-end-full.jpg), and Slagbinder wins average 581 s against Duskblade's 323 s (audit). Long fights for weak classes feel like punishment, not challenge.
11. **Splitting players across too many modes too early.** (Judgment.) Twelve places shown while only the Kiln is open for tiers 1 to 3, and raids that need four people, risk empty queues and confusion at launch.
