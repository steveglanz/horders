# Market research: what makes top mobile action roguelites last, the "AI slop" problem, and how to charge

Research date: 27 Nov 2026. Written for Steve, the owner, in plain English. Every claim about another game or the market has a link. Where a point is **my judgment**, it says so. I didn't play these games for this report. Ratings come from the US, UK or AU App Store pages as I read them today, and they change over time.

---

## The short version

1. **The games that last keep things simple.** You can play them with one or two thumbs. A run fits a short break (about 5 to 30 minutes). Each one has a single look you could recognise from one screenshot. Most importantly, the price is fair and never gets in the way of play. Every long-lived 4.5+ star game below either charges once or makes ads optional. Not one sells power, energy or revives as its main business.
2. **Players dislike generative AI in games, and the dislike is growing.** In a late-2025 survey of 1,799 players, 85% felt negative about it and 63% chose the most negative answer. Art and music were the most disliked uses ([Quantic Foundry](https://quanticfoundry.com/2025/12/18/gen-ai/)). Games with even a small amount of AI art or AI cutscenes have been review-bombed or had to apologise in 2025 and 2026. **Our Suno music is the biggest risk of this kind we have today.** The art made in code is actually an advantage we can tell players about.
3. **The model I'd recommend is "free to try, one payment unlocks the full game", priced around $4.99 to $6.99, plus optional cosmetics and no ads (my judgment).** This is the model Grimvalor uses, and it is close to Shattered Pixel Dungeon and Brotato. The comparable games charge $3 to $15, not $1. Energy timers, paid revives, loot boxes and power for sale are what pull ratings down.

### What they share

**Presentation: you recognise the look at once and can read the action.**
- Each game has **one look you can spot from a single screenshot**. Downwell, made by one person, uses just four colours: black background, white outlines, red for gems, enemies and key items, and blue for water ([Wikipedia summary](https://mobileapps.wikiwand.com/en/Downwell_(video_game))). Dead Cells colours whole areas from grey textures with "gradient maps", so the team can recolour an entire biome cheaply. It keeps "saturated midtones and high-contrast enemies and projectiles" because "threats stand out and your reaction times are naturally better" ([Game Developer, Dead Cells art deep dive](https://gamedeveloper.com/production/art-design-deep-dive-giving-back-colors-to-cryptic-worlds-in-i-dead-cells-i-)).
- Reviewers praise **sound and music as part of the "quality" feel**. Oceanhorn's review leads with its score by famous composers ([TouchArcade](https://toucharcade.com/2013/11/14/oceanhorn-monster-of-uncharted-seas-review-zelda-is-better-without-the-princess/)). The Dead Cells review praises "art, music and combat" together ([TouchArcade](https://toucharcade.com/2019/08/28/dead-cells-iphone-review/)).
- **Small touch targets get criticised.** Slay the Spire's review calls the iPhone targets "too small" and the game "nearly unplayable" on an iPhone SE ([TouchArcade](https://toucharcade.com/2020/06/15/slay-the-spire-ios-review-iphone-ipad-performance-icloud-megacrit-humble-games/)). *Horders:* this matches our audit point that heroes are about 30 px tall and trash enemies are a few pixels on a 390 px phone (13-08-hud.jpg). Small things on screen read as "cheap" and "hard".

**Controls: one thumb, or very few buttons, with aiming done for you as an option.**
- Archero: drag anywhere to move, and the hero shoots the nearest enemy automatically ([Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2019/8/9/why-archero-banked-25m-but-leaves-25m-hanging-hlx9n)). Archero 2: "dragging moves, standing still fires" ([Gaming on Phone](https://www.gamingonphone.com/reviews/archero-2-game-review)).
- Soul Knight aims automatically at the nearest foe ([TouchArcade](https://toucharcade.com/2017/03/10/soul-knight-review/)). Brotato fires automatically by default, with manual aiming as an option ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/brotato-premium/id1668755109)).
- Dead Cells has 3 or 4 main buttons near the screen edge, a floating or fixed stick, and an "auto-hit" option that commenters say makes the touch version much better ([TouchArcade](https://toucharcade.com/2019/08/28/dead-cells-iphone-review/)). Grimvalor starts with three buttons (jump, attack, dash) and adds more as you level up, and its controls are "spot on from the very start" ([Android Central](https://www.androidcentral.com/grimvalor-beautifully-polished-platformer-you-can-try-out-free-game-week)).
- *Horders:* a stick plus 4 ability buttons with an automatic basic attack is in line with Dead Cells and Grimvalor. Unlocking the buttons one per level (Q to F at levels 1 to 4) is the same pattern Grimvalor uses. **My judgment:** keep pressing abilities, since it is one of the things that sets us apart. But make sure every button is thumb-sized, and consider a "smart cast" setting that aims abilities at the current target.

**Session length: one run fits a break, and the game remembers where you were.**
- Archero 2's Survival mode runs "around 6 to 7 minutes" ([Gaming on Phone](https://www.gamingonphone.com/reviews/archero-2-game-review)). Brotato's runs take "under 30 minutes" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/brotato-premium/id1668755109)). Soul Knight is "best in short bursts" ([TouchArcade](https://toucharcade.com/2017/03/10/soul-knight-review/)).
- **Cloud saves matter.** Slay the Spire's reviewer called the missing iCloud sync "the biggest deal-breaker" ([TouchArcade](https://toucharcade.com/2020/06/15/slay-the-spire-ios-review-iphone-ipad-performance-icloud-megacrit-humble-games/)).
- *Horders:* a delve of about 8 to 12 minutes is right in this band, and we already have cloud saves. **My judgment:** add pause and resume mid-delve (if a phone call or app switch loses a run, players punish it in reviews). Also keep bosses under about 90 seconds for every class. Our audit's 581-second Slagbinder wins and the 6-minute Kiln Mother loss are well outside the band.

**Onboarding: a free first hour that shows off the best of the game.**
- Grimvalor's free first act gives "about one to two hours" of play before the unlock ([Android Central](https://www.androidcentral.com/grimvalor-beautifully-polished-platformer-you-can-try-out-free-game-week)).
- Oceanhorn was marked down because the "developer doesn't explain the controls well enough" ([TouchArcade](https://toucharcade.com/2013/11/14/oceanhorn-monster-of-uncharted-seas-review-zelda-is-better-without-the-princess/)).
- Soul Knight was criticised for "no clear information on stats" and "no strong progression hook" ([TouchArcade](https://toucharcade.com/2017/03/10/soul-knight-review/)).
- *Horders:* the audit says our first screen is the delve picker, with no title, no "who am I" moment, and eight status chips on the hub by tier 6 (13-01-hub.jpg). **My judgment:** the strong pattern is a short, authored opening run, the hub opening up one system at a time, and the first 60 to 90 minutes treated as the free "demo" that sells the unlock.

**Monetization: fair, predictable, and never in the way of play.**
- Vampire Survivors' creator called it a "free-for-real approach", with ads that "always be optional and in your control" and with no "real money sinks". He made the mobile version himself because he couldn't find a "non-predatory" mobile partner ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/vampire-survivors-developer-takes-new-approach-to-monetisation), [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/vampire-survivors-studio-says-it-couldnt-find-a-non-predatory-mobile-developer-so-it-did-the-job-itself)).
- Soul Knight's reviewer praised its economy as generous and free of "whale hunting" pricing ([TouchArcade](https://toucharcade.com/2017/03/10/soul-knight-review/)).
- Grimvalor's paid version states "No ads or in-app purchases" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/grimvalor/id1369179088)).

### Warning examples: Archero and Diablo Immortal
- **Archero** is built to sell: a paid revive after death (the abilities you built up make giving up hard), paid chests or ad-gated chests on 24-hour and 7-day timers, and bundles ([Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2019/8/9/why-archero-banked-25m-but-leaves-25m-hanging-hlx9n)). It earned a lot. But a review of the sequel scores its free-to-play side 6.5/10 and says it "does push you toward purchasing" with a "pay-to-win approach" ([Gaming on Phone](https://www.gamingonphone.com/reviews/archero-2-game-review)). That model needs paid user acquisition and live operations, which a small team can't run. That last point is my judgment.
- **Diablo Immortal** shows what happens when a premium-looking game sells power. Its iOS user score was 0.6 from about 1,700 Metacritic reviews against a critic score of 76, and a player estimate put a fully geared character at about $50,000 ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/diablo-immortal-microtransactions-have-sparked-a-brutal-backlash), [VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/diablo-immortal-now-has-the-lowest-user-score-in-metacritic-history)). *Horders:* the lesson for us is never to sell anything in the loot, Forge or embers chain. Our rarity curve ("loot exponentially rarer") is fine as long as money can't speed it up.

### What "replay value" and "the hook" look like in these games (my judgment, drawn from the reviews above)
- **Builds that change how a run plays**, not just bigger numbers: Brotato's six weapons at once, Slay the Spire's decks, Dead Cells' weapon combinations. Our draft cards are mostly "+X" today ("costs 6 less Heat", 13-09-draft.jpg). A few cards per class should change *how* you play.
- **A short, clear long-term ladder** with **one** main loop: Slay the Spire's Ascension levels, Pocket Rogues' floors. We have about ten meta systems competing for attention, so our hub shows tiers *plus* Bounty, Key, Weekly, Seen, Season, Omens and Potion. The games that last surface one or two of these at a time.
- **A daily challenge with a shared seed.** Slay the Spire has one with leaderboards ([TouchArcade](https://toucharcade.com/2020/06/15/slay-the-spire-ios-review-iphone-ipad-performance-icloud-megacrit-humble-games/)), and we already have one. Keep it.

## 2. AI-generated art and music: how players react, the disclosure rules, and how to look distinctive on no budget

### How players react (2024 to 2026)
- **Survey:** in Quantic Foundry's Oct to Dec 2025 survey (1,799 players), **85% felt negative** about generative AI in games and **63% chose "very negative"**. Younger players were the most negative: only 3% of 13 to 17 year olds and 7% of 18 to 24 year olds felt positive. **Art, music and sound, and dialogue drew the most negative responses** ([Quantic Foundry](https://quanticfoundry.com/2025/12/18/gen-ai/)).
- **Small amounts still cause backlash:**
  - *Crimson Desert* (Pearl Abyss, Mar 2026): players found a few AI-made decorative images the day after launch. The studio apologised, added a Steam AI disclosure and promised an audit of every asset ([Engadget](https://engadget.com/gaming/crimson-desert-developer-apologizes-and-promises-to-replace-ai-generated-art-183716439.html)).
  - *Vapor World: Over The Mind* (Aug 2026): about 100K wishlists before launch, then "Mostly Negative" reviews because of AI cutscenes. The top review complained the AI couldn't keep the hero's look consistent ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/steam-week-in-review-a-touch-of-ai-is-all-it-takes-to-trigger-backlash-as-a-promising-new-indie-falls-afoul-of-slop-skeptics/)).
  - *Metal Eden* (Sep 2025): about 10 AI voice lines, disclosed honestly, still started a "punished for honesty" debate ([Gamepressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/newsroom/discussion-about-ai-label-on-steam-continues-the-dev-is-being-pun/ze8ab3)).
  - *1666: Amsterdam* apologised for AI art in its demo ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/1666-amsterdam-apology-ai-assets-panache-genai-2000705525)).
  - Some developers have even been *falsely* accused of using AI ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/rpg-dev-pushes-back-against-steam-review-ai-accusations-we-poured-years-of-our-lives-into-this-game-and-only-worked-with-real-human-artists-on-everything)). So a generic look gets you accused even when nothing was generated.
- **Publishers are taking sides.** Hooded Horse writes a ban on AI art into its publishing contracts and tells developers not to use it even as placeholders, "because one leftover asset can slip into a release" ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/hooded-horse-gen-ai-art-ban-4x-strategy-steam-2000658179)).
- **Music specifically:** a music-licensing executive told a 2026 industry summit she sees "more resistance against any kind of generative music in games", because players and developers "really love the human aspect" ([AI Musicpreneur](https://www.aimusicpreneur.com/ai-music-industry/vickie-nauman-crossborderworks-games-ai-music-resistance-2026/)).
- **But AI use is spreading anyway.** About 20% of Steam games released in 2025 disclosed AI use (7,818 titles, about 7% of the store). About 60% of those disclosures were for visual assets ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/video-games/pc-gaming/1-in-5-steam-games-released-in-2025-use-generative-ai-up-nearly-700-percent-year-on-year-7-818-titles-disclose-genai-asset-usage-7-percent-of-entire-steam-library)). Reports say almost half of new releases disclosed it by Oct 2026 ([Dexerto](https://dexerto.com/gaming/almost-half-of-all-new-steam-games-use-ai-now-3423933)). **My judgment:** this flood makes "no generative AI art or music" a stronger selling point, not a weaker one, because it sets a game apart.

### Disclosure rules
- **Steam:** has required an AI disclosure since Jan 2024, covering both content made before release and content generated while the game runs ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/video-games/pc-gaming/1-in-5-steam-games-released-in-2025-use-generative-ai-up-nearly-700-percent-year-on-year-7-818-titles-disclose-genai-asset-usage-7-percent-of-entire-steam-library)). Valve rewrote the rules in Jan 2026. **AI coding helpers and other development tools don't need disclosing.** Anything AI-made that ships in the game, on the store page or in marketing does, and it appears in an "AI Generated Content Disclosure" section on the store page ([VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/valve-has-significantly-rewritten-steams-rules-for-how-developers-much-disclose-ai-use)). *For Horders on Steam:* Suno music would have to be disclosed. Code written by Claude would not.
- **Google Play:** its AI-Generated Content policy covers apps that *generate* content with AI (chatbots, image generators). It is "not intended to cover" apps that merely contain AI-made material ([Google Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14094294)). So there's no store label on Play today.
- **Apple App Store:** I found no Apple rule that requires disclosing pre-made AI assets (my reading; I didn't find an official statement either way). Apple *does* require loot-box odds to be shown before purchase ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/apple-now-requires-app-store-games-to-disclose-loot-box-odds/)).
- **My judgment:** the mobile stores not requiring a label doesn't protect us. Players find AI content themselves, as with Crimson Desert, and reviews are where the damage happens. Suno songs also have a recognisable sound. Before charging money, either replace the music or decide openly to keep it and say so. Check Suno's commercial-use terms for the paid plan at launch time; I didn't research them here.

### What this means for Horders specifically
- **Music (Suno):** this is our highest "AI slop" risk. Players rate music among the most disliked AI uses ([Quantic Foundry](https://quanticfoundry.com/2025/12/18/gen-ai/)). Options, all my judgment:
  1. Write the music in code, the way the sound effects already are: a small set of motifs for each dungeon in one consistent synth style, so it is cheap, ours and cohesive.
  2. Use free, human-made music licensed CC0 or CC-BY for now, then commission a composer at launch with Steve's budget. A short score of 6 to 10 loops is a modest commission.
  3. Keep Suno and disclose it. Expect some reviews to call it out.
- **Text and names:** our gear names follow a template ("Titanheart Hauberk", sim/content/items.ts:107-132). The audit flags this as the exact pattern players call AI slop. Writing by hand fixes it: fewer items, each with a real name and a line of flavour tied to a place in our world.
- **Art made in code:** none of our art comes from an image generator. **My judgment:** that is a real story to tell ("every sprite and sound is hand-built in code"). But it only helps if the art *looks* deliberate. A generic or inconsistent look gets accused of AI anyway, as the false-accusation cases show.

### How small teams get a cohesive, distinctive look on almost no budget

Examples with sources:
- **Downwell** (one creator): **four colours**, with each colour having one job (red means danger or value) ([Wikipedia summary](https://mobileapps.wikiwand.com/en/Downwell_(video_game))).
- **Thronefall** (two developers, neither a trained artist): "a very limited color palette combined with simple 3D models", so they could add content fast. They added **outlines so silhouettes read clearly**, because silhouettes carry most of the information ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/mastering-minimalism-and-layering-complexity-with-strategy-game-thronefall)).
- **Dead Cells:** grey art coloured by gradient maps, with high-contrast threats. That makes the game cheap to recolour and easy to read ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/production/art-design-deep-dive-giving-back-colors-to-cryptic-worlds-in-i-dead-cells-i-)).
- **KILL KNIGHT** (PlaySide): a low-fidelity, PS1-style look as a deliberate choice. They kept "consistent tonal ranges across all art elements", put clear silhouettes first, and built a visuals-only test slice before full production. They dropped dithering because it read as noise in 3D ([Unity blog](https://unity.com/blog/how-playside-studios-built-kill-knight-visual-identity)).

The common recipe (my judgment, built from the above):
1. **One master palette for the whole game** (about 16 to 32 colours). Each dungeon gets 2 or 3 accent colours from it, instead of a full-screen colour wash. Today each of our dungeons is washed in one strong colour (rooms-sheet-0.png: orange, teal, purple, blue, green), which makes rooms look alike and hides foes.
2. **Colour has a meaning and the whole game sticks to it.** Steve already set green as good and red as bad. Extend that: gold means "stand here", red means "move", a single cool colour means "interruptible". Never use those colours for decoration.
3. **Shape language.** Heroes are built from rounded, stable shapes. Foes are angular, spiky and asymmetric. Bosses are huge versions of their dungeon's motif. A player should know friend from foe by silhouette alone.
4. **Pick ONE rendering style and commit to it.** For a three.js game built by agents, **flat-shaded low-poly 3D with outlines (the Thronefall approach)** is the most realistic. Code can generate it, it scales to every class and foe, and it avoids the current mix of 2D billboards over 3D floors. The other option is to stay 2D and redraw sprites bigger with a strict palette. Mixing the two is what reads as "thrown together".
5. **Bring the camera closer or make characters bigger.** Heroes should be at least about 60 to 80 px tall on a 390 px phone, and trash should be big enough to read as "menacing creatures", which was Steve's own ask.
6. **Build a visuals-only test slice first** (one Kiln room, one hero, one pack, one boss telegraph) and sign it off before redoing all twelve places, as the KILL KNIGHT team did.
7. **Give each place one landmark set piece** so screenshots differ. The audit notes there are no landmark rooms today.

## 3. Which payment models rate well and which rate badly for a small, premium-feeling game

### What the market looks like
- Free games took **96% of mobile game downloads in 2025**. But **premium releases rose 77% in 2025** to nearly 750, after falling from 1,000+ in 2022 to 422 in 2024. Balatro alone made **$21.3M on mobile from 3.1M downloads** (AppMagic data via [Pocket Tactics](https://www.pockettactics.com/premium-mobile-games-increase), July 2026).
- Older developer data (2016): a paid app converts store visitors to downloads about **10 times worse** than a free one (about 0.2% vs 2%), so paid games need featuring or marketing. A "free to level five, then one purchase" model got about **14% of players to pay** ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/indie-mavens-are-paid-apps-dead/)). These figures are old, so treat them as direction, not forecasts.
- Apple allows a **free trial for non-subscription apps** through a $0 in-app purchase, as long as the app says what ends and what the full price is ([iLounge](https://www.ilounge.com/index.php/news/comments/revised-app-store-review-guidelines-now-allow-for-free-trials)). A plain "free download plus a one-time unlock purchase" is also standard, as Grimvalor shows.

### How each model scores

| Model | Examples and ratings | How players and reviewers react | Fit for Horders (my judgment) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Paid up front** ($3 to $15) | Shattered PD iOS 4.9 ($4.99); Brotato Premium 4.7 ($4.99); Grimvalor 4.8 (£5.99); Pocket Rogues 4.7 ($2.99); Dead Cells about 4.5 ($8.99); Slay the Spire 4.4 to 4.5 ($9.99 to $14.99) | Ratings are the highest, because only people who chose the game rate it. Hard to get discovered, about 10 times fewer downloads | Good for ratings, risky for reach with no marketing budget |
| **Free + one-time unlock (demo, then pay)** | Grimvalor on Android (first act free, $6.99 unlock); Glyph Quest (about 14% paid) | Seen as fair. Developers call it "not completely locking out players who can't (or just won't) pay" ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/indie-mavens-are-paid-apps-dead/)). Opinion pieces call it a discovery tool too ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/iap-strategies-most-mobile-gamers-don-t-hate-and-most-aaa-publishers-should-try)) | **Best fit.** Our first hour (Kiln plus the first unlocks) is a natural demo |
| **Free with optional reward ads only** | Vampire Survivors 4.6; Soul Knight (generous) | Accepted when ads are truly optional and never break up play ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/vampire-survivors-developer-takes-new-approach-to-monetisation)) | Earns little at our scale. Ads in a "premium feel" game clash with the look |
| **Two versions (free with ads plus a paid one)** | Brotato: both versions 4.7, but the free one sells a $19.99 "No Ads" and $15.99 VIP | Works, but reviewers call some free-version items a "cash grab" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/brotato/id6445884925)) | Doubles store and support work. Not for 1.0 |
| **Cosmetics only** (skins, glows, titles) | Common as add-ons; Soul Knight sells $1 characters | Rarely criticised when it gives no power | Fine as a small extra on top of the unlock (for example a class skin pack). Our season glows and titles are already cosmetic |
| **Small DLC / expansions** | Vampire Survivors ($0.99 to $3.99), Brotato Abyssal Terrors ($2.99) | Well received when the base game is complete | A good after-launch path: new dungeon or class packs |
| **Energy, paid revives, chests, power packs, battle pass for power** | Archero (packs up to $14.99, revive purchase, timed chests); Archero 2's free-to-play side rated 6.5/10, "pay-to-win" | The model players punish most. Diablo Immortal: 0.6 user score | **Avoid.** It would wreck the premium feel, and Steve's "loot exponentially rarer" plan would become a pay wall |
| **Loot boxes** | Archero chests | Apple requires the odds to be shown ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/apple-now-requires-app-store-games-to-disclose-loot-box-odds/)). Widely disliked, and regulated in some countries | Avoid |

### Price point
- The comparable premium games sit between **$2.99 and $9.99** on iOS (table above). One developer in the 2016 roundup said paid indies "underprice" and should charge more ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/indie-mavens-are-paid-apps-dead/)).
- **My judgment:** $0.99 tells players the game is throwaway and won't fund anything. That clashes with the "premium feel" Steve wants. A **$4.99 to $6.99 unlock** after a generous free first hour fits the market best. At launch, a short discount (say $2.99 for the first week) is fine.

### My recommendation for Horders (judgment)
1. **Free download. The tutorial Kiln plus tiers 1 to 3, with one or two classes, are free.** One purchase, around $4.99 to $6.99, unlocks every class, dungeon, raid, the tier ladder and co-op hosting. Free players can still *join* a friend's co-op (assuming the netcode allows it), which spreads the game by word of mouth.
2. **No ads at all, at least in 1.0.** That gives a cleaner premium feel and less SDK and privacy work.
3. **Optional cosmetics later** (class skins, weapon glows), never power, never randomised.
4. **After launch:** paid expansion packs (a new dungeon, or a new class) priced $1.99 to $3.99, like Vampire Survivors and Brotato.
5. **Never sell** embers, loot, Forge upgrades, revives, keys or time skips.
6. On the store page and title screen: "One price. No ads. No energy. Every sprite and sound hand-built." This only holds if the music question is settled first.
